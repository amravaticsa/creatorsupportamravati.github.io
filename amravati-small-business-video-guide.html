Plan Better Videos for Your Small Business

A no-nonsense planning guide for local shops, clinics, educators, service providers, creators and community organisations that want useful video content—not just random posts.

By Creator Support Amravati Editorial Team · Updated 25 November 2026 · Editorial policy

A good business video starts before the camera is switched on. Decide who you want to reach, what they need to know, what proof you can show, and what action should follow. This guide turns those decisions into a simple production plan. It is educational guidance, not a promise of views, leads or sales.

In this guide1. Choose one clear goal2. Pick a format that fits3. Build a simple story4. Prepare for the shoot5. Publish accessibly6. Review useful signals7. Copy-ready checklist

1. Choose one clear goal

Do not try to explain every service in one 30-second reel. Pick one job for each video: introduce the business, explain one product, answer a common customer question, show a process, share a real customer experience, or announce an event.

Awareness

Show what the business does, where it serves customers and what makes the experience distinctive.

Consideration

Demonstrate a product, explain a service process, compare options fairly or answer frequent questions.

Trust

Use authentic team introductions, behind-the-scenes footage and customer feedback only with permission.

Action

Give one next step: call, visit, request a quote, book a consultation or read a detailed page.

2. Pick a format that fits

  • Vertical reel (9:16): a short demonstration, quick tip, product feature or one-question answer.
  • Customer story: a real customer explains their experience in their own words; get written permission before publishing.
  • Explainer: a presenter walks through a process, service or frequently asked question.
  • Event recap: a concise summary of an event, with consent for identifiable attendees where required.
  • Website video: a fuller introduction or demonstration for visitors who want more detail.

Choose the format based on the audience and distribution channel. Do not assume every platform or campaign needs the same edit.

3. Build a simple story

Try this structure for a short video:

  1. Opening: state the customer question or problem in plain language.
  2. Useful detail: show the product, process, people or evidence that answers it.
  3. Context: add only the details a viewer needs to understand the offer.
  4. Next step: give one clear call to action and a way to contact the business.

Use real claims that the business can support. Avoid invented testimonials, misleading before-and-after comparisons, guaranteed outcomes or unsupported superlatives.

4. Prepare for the shoot

Location and access

Confirm the address, permission to film, available time, customer flow, power and any restricted areas.

People and consent

Tell participants how footage will be used. Obtain appropriate consent and avoid filming customers or minors without proper permission.

Sound and light

Reduce background noise, check microphone levels, control harsh backlight and record a short test clip.

Brand assets

Keep the current logo, correct business name, contact details, product information and approved brand colours ready.

5. Publish accessibly

  • Add accurate captions; check names, prices, addresses and technical terms before publishing.
  • Keep important text away from platform interface overlays and ensure it is readable on a phone.
  • Use a clear thumbnail or opening frame that matches the actual video.
  • Write a useful description and include a relevant destination page, not an unrelated homepage link.
  • Use music, stock footage, logos and images only when you have the necessary rights or permission.
  • Provide contact and booking details consistently with the business website and Google Business Profile.

6. Review useful signals

Compare each video against its purpose. For awareness, review reach and relevant audience retention. For consideration, examine saves, shares and visits to the related service page. For enquiries, track qualified calls, forms or bookings. Record the date range and platform, and avoid attributing every sale to one post without evidence.

Use the findings to improve the next video: opening clarity, sound, pacing, captions, audience fit or call to action. A single view count is not a complete performance report.

7. Copy-ready pre-production checklist

  • One primary goal and intended audience are written down
  • Video format, platform and approximate duration are decided
  • Key message, script points and call to action are approved
  • Location, schedule and access permissions are confirmed
  • Participants know where and how the footage may be used
  • Logo, product details and contact information are current
  • Music, footage and other assets have usage permission
  • Captions, review process and final deliverables are agreed
  • Success measures and reporting period are selected
