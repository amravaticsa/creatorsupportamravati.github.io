Business Reels Editing: A Practical Brief Before You Hire an Editor

Turn raw clips into a clear, useful short video for your audience.

By: Creator Support Amravati · Updated: 14 November 2026

A business Reel should do more than use fast cuts and trending audio. It should help the right viewer understand a product, service, offer, place or customer experience. Before sending footage to an editor, prepare a short brief so the final video has a clear purpose.

1. Choose one goal for the Reel

Decide whether the video should introduce your business, explain one service, show a product feature, announce an offer, share a customer experience or invite people to visit or enquire. Avoid trying to communicate every detail in one short video.

2. Write the opening hook

The first few seconds should make the topic clear. For example, a restaurant might open with the dish being served; a jewellery store might show a close-up detail; a clinic might introduce a common patient question in a responsible, non-misleading way. The hook should match what the video actually delivers.

3. Record useful footage

  • Capture a mix of wide, medium and close-up shots.
  • Keep the camera stable and clean the lens before recording.
  • Record each important action for a few extra seconds.
  • Use consistent lighting and avoid strong backlight where possible.
  • Record clean speech separately or close to the microphone when dialogue matters.
  • Capture vertical footage when the primary deliverable is an Instagram Reel.

4. Give the editor a clear sequence

Share the must-use shots, preferred order and any moments to avoid. If you do not know the exact sequence, explain the story: problem, product or service, proof or demonstration, and next step. Do not send dozens of unrelated clips without context.

5. Prepare brand assets and accurate text

Send the latest logo, correct business name, address or contact details if they need to appear, brand colours and approved offer wording. Check spelling and prices before editing begins. Do not ask the editor to guess business facts or invent customer reviews.

6. Confirm music, captions and format

Decide whether the Reel needs subtitles, on-screen labels, voice-over, sound effects or background music. Use music and other assets only when you have the necessary rights for the intended use. Confirm vertical 9:16 delivery and whether you also need a clean version without text or a different cut for another platform.

7. Agree on revisions and delivery

Before work begins, agree on the final duration, number of Reels, first-preview date, delivery format, file-sharing method and correction rounds. Send consolidated feedback with timestamps—for example, “00:08: replace this shot”—rather than multiple conflicting messages.

Example: a simple Reel structure for a local business

For a product or service introduction, a practical sequence could be: (1) a close-up or action shot that makes the subject clear, (2) one or two shots showing the product or service in use, (3) a short supporting detail such as a feature, location or process, and (4) a clear next step such as visiting the store or sending an enquiry. Adapt the sequence to the business; do not add claims, offers or customer endorsements that the business has not approved.

Quality check before publishing

  • Important text stays inside safe areas and is not hidden by app interface buttons.
  • Captions match the spoken words and remain on screen long enough to read.
  • Music and footage are cleared for the intended commercial use.
  • The business name, contact details, offer and call to action are accurate.
  • The exported video is watched from beginning to end on a mobile device.

FAQs

How many clips should I send for one Reel?

There is no fixed number. Send enough relevant footage to cover the story, plus a short note identifying the strongest shots. Quality and relevance matter more than sending many random clips.

Should I add trending music to every business Reel?

No. Choose audio that fits the message and that you are permitted to use for the account and intended commercial purpose. Clear voice and product sound may be more useful.

Can one video be used on Instagram and YouTube Shorts?

Often the same vertical master can be adapted, but check platform-specific text placement, audio rights, captions and export requirements.
