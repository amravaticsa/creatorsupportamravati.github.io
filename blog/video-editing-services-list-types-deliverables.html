Video Editing Services List: What Each Type Includes and When You Need It

By Creator Support Amravati · Video Production & Editing Guide · Updated 25 November 2026

“Video editing” can describe anything from trimming a short phone clip to finishing a multi-camera brand film. The right service depends on the purpose of the video, the footage available and where the finished content will be published. This guide explains the main types of video editing, what a typical project may include and what to clarify before hiring an editor.

Why video editing services are not all the same

A creator preparing a weekly YouTube episode has different needs from a business launching a new product or a family preserving a wedding film. Each project has its own audience, pace, visual language and delivery requirements. A useful service description should therefore explain the work involved rather than simply promise “professional editing”.

Some projects need only clean cuts and balanced sound. Others require footage selection, a clear narrative, colour correction, motion graphics, subtitles, multiple camera angles and several versions for different platforms. Understanding these differences helps you request the right service and compare quotations fairly.

1. Reels, Shorts and vertical social media editing

Short-form videos are designed to communicate quickly. Editing usually begins by finding the strongest moment or idea and placing it near the beginning. Unnecessary pauses are removed, the framing is adjusted for a vertical screen and captions are added where they improve understanding. Music, sound effects and simple motion graphics can support the message, but they should not distract from the subject.

This service is useful for creators, coaches, local businesses, professionals and brands that publish regularly. A longer recording can sometimes be divided into several short clips, but each cut should have its own context and opening. Simply splitting a long video into equal pieces does not automatically create engaging short-form content.

2. YouTube and long-form video editing

A YouTube video needs more than a sequence of cuts. The editor may shape the structure, remove repeated explanations, tighten pauses, add supporting B-roll and use graphics to clarify important points. For interviews or educational videos, chapter markers and on-screen headings can help viewers follow the subject. Consistent audio levels are particularly important when a video contains several speakers or was recorded in different locations.

Before starting, agree on the expected final duration and whether the editor is responsible for selecting highlights, adding background music, creating an intro or preparing short promotional clips from the full episode. Thumbnail design and upload management are separate deliverables unless they are specifically included in the project scope.

3. Podcast video and audio post-production

Podcast editing can involve syncing separate camera recordings with microphone audio, switching between speakers, removing distracting gaps and balancing dialogue. A multi-camera conversation may look more natural when the edit changes angle at meaningful moments rather than cutting randomly. The final episode can also be used to create short clips, quote-led videos and vertical highlights for social platforms.

Audio deserves special attention. Different microphones may capture different volume levels, room noise or echo. An editor can improve clarity within the limits of the recording, but severe clipping or very poor source audio cannot always be fully repaired. Recording clean audio at the shoot stage makes post-production more reliable.

4. Business profile, brand film and company introduction videos

A business video should explain what the organisation does, who it serves and why the viewer should care. Editing may include selecting the strongest footage, arranging a clear story, adding the company name, service information, location details and a relevant call to action. The pace should match the brand: a professional service introduction may need calm, confident editing, while a product launch can use a faster visual rhythm.

These videos are often used on websites, presentations, social media and sales conversations. It is worth preparing approved spellings, contact details, logos and any claims that need verification before the edit begins. This reduces avoidable corrections near delivery.

5. Product videos and advertisement editing

Product editing is built around showing the item clearly and communicating its useful features. Close-up shots, demonstrations, text callouts, voice-over and carefully selected music can help viewers understand the product. An advertisement may also need alternate lengths or aspect ratios for different placements. The editor should receive the approved offer, price, dates and product claims from the client rather than guessing or inventing them.

For paid campaigns, the first few seconds and the clarity of the message matter. A visually attractive video is not enough if the viewer cannot understand the offer or what to do next. Keep the call to action specific and make sure the final text remains readable on a mobile screen.

6. Wedding, event and highlight film editing

Event editing often involves a large volume of footage recorded over several hours. The task is to identify meaningful moments and create a coherent film rather than include every recorded clip. For a wedding, this may mean combining preparations, ceremony highlights, family moments and the celebration into a story that reflects the event. For a conference or public programme, the edit may focus on speakers, audience response, key announcements and the overall atmosphere.

Multiple cameras, changing lighting and live audio can make event post-production more demanding. Names, titles, dates and permissions should be checked carefully, especially when the finished video will be published publicly.

7. Colour correction, colour grading and sound design

Colour correction and creative grading are related but different tasks. Correction aims to make footage look balanced: exposure, white balance and shot-to-shot consistency are adjusted. Grading adds a deliberate visual character while keeping skin tones and important details believable. A preset or LUT can be a starting point, but it cannot correct every lighting or exposure problem automatically.

Sound work may include dialogue balancing, noise reduction, music placement and sound effects. The goal is not to make every sound louder; it is to make the important information clear and the overall experience comfortable to watch.

What should a video editing quotation include?

A clear quotation should identify the source footage, approximate raw duration, expected final runtime, number of deliverables, aspect ratios, graphics or subtitle requirements, deadline and revision process. It should also state whether licensed music, stock footage, voice-over, thumbnail design, project files or upload support are included. These details make it easier to compare two offers that may appear similar at first.

For recurring content, discuss how many videos are expected each month, how footage will be transferred, who approves scripts and captions, and how urgent requests will be handled. A repeatable workflow usually saves time for both the client and editor.

How to prepare a useful editing brief

Send the original footage wherever possible, along with a short explanation of the audience and purpose. Mention the platform, preferred style, reference examples, target length, language, logo, brand colours and any sections that must be included or avoided. If you have a specific deadline, communicate it before the work is scheduled. The more clearly the project is described, the more accurately its scope can be discussed.

How to choose the right deliverables

Start with where the video will be used and what the viewer should do next. A vertical social clip may need captions and a quick opening; a training video may need chapter markers and clean screen recordings; a corporate film may need several aspect ratios and approved subtitles. Specify the deliverable before editing begins so the source footage can be planned accordingly.

Do not assume every extra is included

Music licensing, stock footage, translation, voice-over, animation, rush delivery, additional revisions and project-file handover may be priced separately. Ask for these items in the written estimate and confirm that third-party licences cover the channels and territories where the video will be published.

Frequently asked questions

What is usually included in basic video editing?

Basic editing commonly covers selecting and arranging clips, trimming, simple transitions and exporting. Captions, complex graphics, advanced audio repair and multiple versions may be additional work.

Can one video be used on every social media platform?

Sometimes, but not always. Different placements may need different aspect ratios, lengths, text-safe areas and opening moments. A platform-specific version can be more effective than cropping one master file everywhere.

Is shooting included in video editing services?

Not automatically. Shooting creates the footage; editing turns that footage into a finished video. Some studios provide both, while others accept client-supplied footage. Confirm the scope before booking.

How do I know which service I need?

Start with the outcome: a Reel, full YouTube episode, podcast, product ad, company profile or event film. Then describe the footage you have and the result you want. A provider can use those details to recommend a suitable workflow.
