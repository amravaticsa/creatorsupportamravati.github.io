Video Editing Ke Liye Raw Footage Kaise Prepare Karein: Upload Checklist

Prepare original camera files, audio, folders, project notes and usage permissions before sending footage to an editor.

By Creator Support Amravati · Video Editing Knowledge Hub · Updated 15 November 2026

Sending footage to a video editor should not feel confusing. A simple folder structure, original files, a short project brief and clear permissions can prevent missing clips, wrong versions and avoidable delays. Use this guide before transferring footage for a YouTube video, podcast, product shoot, event highlight, course lesson or social media Reel.

In this guide
  1. Keep original files
  2. Use a clear folder structure
  3. Write an editing brief
  4. Transfer large files safely
  5. Check privacy and usage rights
  6. Final hand-off checklist

1. Keep the original files

Whenever possible, send the original recordings from the camera or phone rather than a copy downloaded from a social platform or messaging app. Some apps compress video, change the audio or reduce resolution. If the original recording is unavailable, tell the editor how the file was created and whether it has already been compressed.

Do not overwrite the memory card or delete the source files immediately after upload. Keep at least one backup until the final edit is approved. If you recorded in multiple sessions, identify which files belong to each session and mention any missing or corrupted clip before editing begins.

2. Use a clear folder structure

Folder names should be short and understandable. Separate video, audio, photographs, logos, scripts and references. For a multi-camera shoot, keep each camera's original files in its own folder. Avoid mixing final exports with raw footage.

Project_Name/
  01_Camera_A/
  02_Camera_B/
  03_External_Audio/
  04_Photos_and_Broll/
  05_Logo_and_Brand_Assets/
  06_Script_and_OnScreen_Text/
  07_References/
  08_Approved_Music/

If you have multiple versions of a logo or script, identify the approved one. A file called “final-final-new2” is easy to misunderstand. Use clear names such as “brand-logo-approved.png” or “lesson-03-script-v2-approved.pdf”. Keep the original camera filenames if the camera workflow depends on them.

3. Write an editing brief the editor can act on

A short brief is more useful than a long message with no priorities. Explain who the video is for, what the viewer should understand and where the final video will be published. Include target duration, language, aspect ratio, deadline and deliverables. Tell the editor what must remain unchanged, such as legal disclaimers, product specifications, a teacher's explanation or a guest's complete answer.

Copy-ready project brief

Project type: YouTube / Podcast / Reel / Product / Event

Audience and purpose: Who should watch and what should they learn or do?

Language: Marathi / Hindi / English / Other

Platform and format: YouTube 16:9 / vertical 9:16 / other

Final output: Runtime, number of videos and any short clips

Style reference: Links with timestamps and what you like about them

Must keep / must remove: Important sections and exclusions

Deadline: Date and any fixed publishing time

For detailed corrections, use timestamps: “02:18 – show the product close-up” or “06:40 – correct the spelling of the speaker's surname”. This is clearer than “the middle part needs improvement”.

4. Transfer large files safely

Choose a transfer method that supports the file size and gives a clear upload-complete confirmation. A slow or unstable connection can interrupt large transfers. After upload, check the file count and open a sample from the destination if the service allows it. If you are sending files in batches, label them Batch 1 of 3, Batch 2 of 3 and Batch 3 of 3, and confirm when the final batch is complete.

Do not assume that a cloud link is private just because it is difficult to guess. Check its access settings and share it only with the intended recipient. Never publish raw client footage in a public folder unless you have explicit permission.

5. Check privacy and usage rights

Before sharing footage, confirm that you have the right to provide it for editing. This is especially important for customer testimonials, interviews, children, private events, workplace recordings and identifiable personal information. Obtain the necessary permissions from people who appear or speak in the video. Do not send unrelated identity documents, passwords or confidential records.

Music, stock footage, fonts, photographs and logos may have usage restrictions. Tell the editor whether you are supplying approved assets or expect the editor to source licensed material. A song being available in a personal social-media account does not automatically mean it can be used in a commercial advertisement.

6. Final hand-off checklist

  • Original footage copied and playable
  • All camera folders and external audio included
  • Logo, photos, slides and approved text attached
  • Project brief includes audience, platform and purpose
  • Target duration, aspect ratio and deliverables confirmed
  • Important timestamps and spelling supplied
  • Deadline and revision process agreed
  • Permissions and usage rights checked
  • Backup of original files retained

Frequently asked questions

Should I send original footage or WhatsApp videos?

Original camera or phone files are preferable. Messaging apps may compress media. If only a forwarded copy is available, tell the editor so expectations about quality can be set correctly.

What if my upload is too large?

Ask the editing team which transfer method and batch size they support. Split the transfer into clearly named batches if needed, and do not delete the original files until receipt is confirmed.

Do I need to send a script?

A script is helpful for educational, promotional and voice-over videos. For interviews and events, a topic outline, guest details and important timestamps may be more practical.

Can I share private customer or event footage?

Only when you have the necessary permission and a suitable private transfer method. Share only the files required for the project and clarify who may access them.
